## Draft narrative structure

### Context

A compact bingo-style playable needed to preserve its interaction and visual hierarchy across portrait and landscape states. The exercise joined implementation and review instead of treating QA as a single final pass.

### Risk map

The review centered on meaningful game objects and states, including board presentation, control reach, typography and numeric readability, responsive layout, progression feedback, the end-user journey, call-to-action behavior, and variant or platform-specific constraints.

### Engineering approach

Ino owned the PixiJS implementation, responsive rules, runtime behavior, and technical corrections. On suitable states, an internal development-integrated Layout Tool supported inspection and saved layout adjustment. The tool is not a public product, external certification system, or provider-acceptance mechanism.

### Quality approach

Olena applied risk-based QA, refined acceptance criteria, and reviewed layout, scale, fonts, responsive states, visual hierarchy, and the end-user journey. Findings were documented with the state and reproduction context, coordinated with engineering, then retested across affected and related states.

### Evidence pattern

The case study should eventually show only approved, reproducible evidence:

1. The intended state and acceptance criterion.
2. The observed issue with orientation and viewport context.
3. The engineering correction or saved layout change.
4. Retest evidence for the corrected and related states.
5. The final disposition, including any remaining limitation.

### Outcome language

Until stronger evidence is approved, the safe outcome is process-level only: the exercise produced a clearer record of reviewed states, corrections, retest status, and remaining release risk. Do not convert this into a commercial or performance claim.
